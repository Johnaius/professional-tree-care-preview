01

Know what is growing

Describe the height and density of the growth and any woody stems. Brush hogging is not the same as removing mature trees, stumps, or all roots.

02

Look at the land

Mention slopes, wet areas, rocks, old fencing, and other known obstacles. Equipment suitability needs to be confirmed before any work is scheduled.

03

Set realistic expectations

A rough-cut area will not have the finish of a maintained lawn. Ask what material remains and what follow-up maintenance may be needed.

BEFORE YOU CALL

Common questions.

Is brush hogging the same as land clearing?+

No. Brush hogging generally cuts rough vegetation. Land clearing can involve trees, stumps, roots, and grading. Describe the desired result so the company can confirm what it can offer.

Can any overgrown lot be brush hogged?+

No. Terrain, ground conditions, access, vegetation size, and hidden obstacles can limit equipment use. An assessment may be needed.

Is brush hogging available in my town?+
